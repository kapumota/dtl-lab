Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, "Reproducible Multi-Layer Evaluation of a Cross-Shard Commit Protocol," for publication in the Research Papers track of Science of Computer Programming, with Formal Techniques as the primary line of work.

The manuscript studies an executable cross-shard commit protocol through a reproducible multi-layer evaluation workflow. It combines bounded property verification using complementary TLA+ and Alloy models, targeted mutation-based property validation, bounded implementation-model trace conformance, and verification-cost characterization under a frozen experimental protocol. The study also reports an independent reproduction of the analytical artifact from a separate clone and workspace.

The work fits the scope of Science of Computer Programming through its pragmatic use of formal techniques together with implementation-level and experimental evidence. Its contribution is the integration of these evidence layers for one executable protocol while keeping their individual limits explicit. In particular, the manuscript does not claim an unbounded correctness proof, general refinement, behavioral equivalence, absolute tool superiority, or an asymptotic scaling law.

The definitive campaign scheduled 1,272 tasks. No property violations were observed among the completed bounded configurations; all 10 predefined scientific mutants were detected; and the evaluated valid and deliberately corrupted trace populations produced the expected conformance outcomes. The verification-cost analysis also identifies the practical boundary of the evaluated configurations, including systematic timeout censoring for the large TLC profile.

A reproducibility package is being finalized with the source code, formal models, frozen experiment specification, preserved observations, derived analysis artifacts, reproduction scripts, and integrity manifests. The final persistent artifact identifier will be supplied before submission.
